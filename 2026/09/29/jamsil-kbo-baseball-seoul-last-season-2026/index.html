Korean Baseball Is Different: Chimaek, Cheer Songs & a Night in Seoul

At Jamsil, chicken and beer, player-specific cheer songs and synchronized chanting can be the main event even if you barely follow baseball. The current stadium is also in its final season.

Published September 29, 2026 · Last checked September 29, 2026

At Jamsil, you can spend an evening eating fried chicken, drinking beer, learning a batter’s cheer song on the fly, and shouting with thousands of people whose team you may not even know. For a first-time visitor, that can be more memorable than the baseball itself.

There is also a one-time reason to go in 2026: LG Twins and Doosan Bears are scheduled to use the current Jamsil Baseball Stadium for the final season before moving to a temporary venue in 2027.

The stands are half the show

If you picture baseball as three hours of quietly watching pitches, Korean professional baseball can be a surprise.

Korea Tourism Organization describes Korean ballparks as a synchronized cheering culture: teams have anthems, individual players have their own songs and chants, and the stands sing and move together. You do not need to study the songs beforehand. If you sit near the cheering section, the simplest strategy is to watch the cheer squad and copy everyone around you.

You can arrive knowing none of the players and still have something to sing, eat and do within the first inning.

Dinner happens in your seat

Chicken and beer — chimaek — is the classic combination, and the official Korea tourism guide specifically calls it a signature Korean ballpark experience. But it does not stop there. Stadium food can include tteokbokki, pizza, skewers and other Korean snacks, so dinner can simply happen during the game instead of before it.

That changes the mood completely. You are not sitting through a sporting event and then going out afterward. The food, drinks, cheering and game are the night out.

Every batter gets a soundtrack

One of the most distinctive parts of KBO culture is that individual players can have their own cheer songs. When a batter comes up, the cheering section switches into another chant and thousands of people follow along.

You do not have to understand Korean to participate. Korea Tourism Organization’s own guide tells first-timers to simply go with the flow, join the applause and follow the crowd. That is why a KBO game can work even for someone who normally finds baseball slow.

Between pitches, the crowd keeps the night moving

The synchronized songs and dances are only part of it. Korean baseball fandom also overlaps with pop-culture habits: team jerseys, customized uniforms, player photo cards and cheering goods are all part of the scene. During breaks, stadium entertainment can include audience-participation moments such as dance battles or “kiss time,” depending on the game.

So if your Seoul itinerary already has palaces, cafés and observation decks, a baseball night gives you something those places do not: three hours inside a loud, participatory Korean crowd.

Do not assume every game starts at 6:30

KBO game times change by day and season period, so do not build your Jamsil evening around a generic “night game” assumption. Under KBO’s current 2026 late-season schedule rules, games from September 15 onward normally start at 18:30 on weekdays, 17:00 on Saturdays, and 14:00 on Sundays and public holidays. Rainouts and doubleheaders can change the schedule.

For example: the official KBO schedule lists the September 29 NC vs Doosan game at Jamsil for 18:30. Always check your exact date on the official KBO schedule before deciding when to eat, arrive or book something afterward.

For the example flow below, “60–90 minutes early” is a Korea, Checked. first-timer buffer for finding the entrance, getting food and settling in—not an official stadium gate-opening rule.

What a first night at Jamsil can look like

This is an example flow, not a fixed timetable. Match times, crowds and food lines vary by game.

About 60–90 minutes before first pitch: arrive while the night is still building

Sports Complex Station starts filling with people in team colors. This is the easiest time to orient yourself, find the correct entrance, look at jerseys and cheering goods, and buy food before the busiest part of the evening.

Before the game: make dinner part of the plan

Do not treat food as something you need to finish before entering. Chicken and beer are part of the classic ballpark experience, and other stadium snacks give you something to share through the early innings.

First innings: you realize the crowd is the second show

The first surprise for many visitors is how quickly the atmosphere changes when a player comes up to bat. Songs switch, the cheer squad leads the section and the crowd answers back. You do not need to know the lyrics. Watch the motions, clap when everyone claps and copy the chorus when it repeats.

Mid-game: the rules matter less than you expected

By this point, even a first-timer can usually recognize the rhythm: batter comes up, a new song starts, food gets passed around, people stand or sit with the flow, and short entertainment breaks reset the energy. This is the moment Korean baseball stops feeling like a sport you need to understand first.

After the final inning: the night continues outside the stadium

Expect a large crowd leaving at once. If you are not rushing, letting the first subway wave pass can make the exit less stressful. After the game, treat the trip home as part of a major-event night rather than expecting an empty station five minutes after the final inning.

Why it works as a Seoul date night

A KBO game is a particularly easy date if you want something more interactive than dinner but less formal than a performance.

  • You do not have to carry the conversation for three hours.
  • You can choose food together and eat in your seats.
  • The crowd gives you an excuse to shout, sing and be ridiculous together.
  • There are natural photo moments: jerseys, cheering gear, food and the stadium itself.
  • Even if the game is slow, the cheering section keeps changing what is happening around you.

Bad date choice: you want a quiet night where you can talk continuously.
Good date choice: you want energy, food and a shared activity without needing to plan every hour.

For the full experience, sit near the cheering section

2026 is the last season in the current Jamsil stadium

Seoul Metropolitan Government says LG Twins and Doosan Bears will use the current Jamsil Baseball Stadium through the 2026 season. From 2027 through 2031, their home games are scheduled to move to a remodeled Jamsil Main Stadium while the new dome is built.

That does not mean Korean baseball disappears. It means the specific old-school Jamsil setting you can experience now has a deadline.

Sort out the ticket first

If the game is a must-do part of your trip, confirm the exact matchup and ticket route before planning the rest of the evening. Walk-up purchases can work, but popular games do sell out.

For a first Seoul trip

Jamsil earns its place in a first Seoul itinerary when you want one evening that feels social rather than sightseeing-heavy. You can eat in the stands, pick up a chant halfway through and spend a few hours inside the crowd instead of looking at the city from outside it.
