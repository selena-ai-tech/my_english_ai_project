How many Seoul trips make the Climate Card cheaper than T-money?

For a 1-day pass, the simple base-fare break-even is 4 separately charged journeys if you already own the Climate Card, or 6 if you need to buy the ₩3,000 physical Climate Card that day.

But do not count every bus or subway boarding. T-money transfers can be free, so count separately fare-charged journeys. And if your route falls outside Climate Card coverage, the price calculation can stop mattering entirely.

1-DAY PASS · CARD ALREADY OWNED

4+

separately charged base-fare journeys

4 × ₩1,550 = ₩6,200, which is above the ₩5,000 1-day pass.

1-DAY PASS · BUYING THE ₩3,000 CARD TODAY

6+

separately charged base-fare journeys

6 × ₩1,550 = ₩9,300, which is above the ₩8,000 first-day Climate Card cost (₩5,000 pass + ₩3,000 card).

Important: “6 trips” does not mean 6 taps or 6 vehicles.

If you take a bus and then transfer to the subway under T-money’s transfer rules, that can remain one fare-charged journey rather than two new ₩1,550 fares. For this comparison, count the times a new base fare is actually charged, not every boarding.

If you need to buy the physical card, use the second threshold.

For first-time visitors, the right-hand column includes the ₩3,000 physical Climate Card purchase. The T-money card purchase price is not added to the T-money side because Seoul says T-money card prices vary by card. That makes these first-time Climate Card thresholds conservative: if you also need to buy a T-money card, the Climate Card can become competitive slightly sooner.

What these numbers mean: they are the first journey counts where simple T-money math at the current ₩1,550 adult subway base fare exceeds the pass cost. They are not guaranteed real-world break-even points.

Now check the three things that can overturn the math.

1. Transfers: count fare-charged journeys, not boardings

Seoul Metropolitan Government’s current fare guidance says integrated transfer fares apply when card-tap and transfer-time rules are met, with up to five free transfers; distance-based charges can still apply. If your “six rides” are really three fare-charged journeys plus transfers, T-money may still cost much less than the simple six-fare example.

2. Coverage: a cheap pass is useless for an uncovered route

The Climate Card does not cover every train and bus around Seoul. Current Seoul guidance excludes the Shinbundang Line, intercity/airport buses, non-Seoul-licensed buses, and subway travel outside the service area. For AREX, the normal covered section is Gimpo International Airport to Seoul Station.

Flying into Incheon? Do not count your airport-to-Seoul AREX ride as a Climate Card ride. Seoul currently allows Climate Card users traveling from the covered area to exit at Incheon Airport T1/T2, but you cannot board AREX at T1/T2 with the Climate Card.

3. Validity: a 1-day pass is not a rolling 24-hour pass

Short-term Climate Card validity begins on the day you load it and runs through the last bus/subway of that calendar-service day. Seoul says short-term passes cannot be loaded in advance. If you load a 1-day pass late at night, you do not get 24 hours from that moment.

Can foreign visitors pay with an overseas card?

Yes, for short-term Climate Cards at supported Seoul Metro vending machines. Seoul Metropolitan Government says that from March 17, 2026, internationally issued credit and debit cards such as Visa and Mastercard can be used to purchase and recharge 1-, 2-, 3-, 5-, and 7-day Climate Card passes at 440 new vending machines across 273 stations on Seoul Metro Lines 1–8.

Seoul also says an average service fee of about 3.7% applies when paying with an internationally issued card. So if you are comparing tiny price differences, include that fee rather than assuming the listed pass price is your exact card charge.

How the break-even was calculated

Current adult subway base fare with a transportation card: ₩1,550 (Seoul Metropolitan Government, effective Jun. 28, 2025).

Short-term Climate Card prices: ₩5,000 / ₩8,000 / ₩10,000 / ₩15,000 / ₩20,000 for 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 7 days. A new physical Climate Card costs ₩3,000.

T-money simple cost = 1,550 × separately charged base-fare journeys

Climate Card cost if already owned = pass price

Climate Card cost if buying a physical card = pass price + 3,000

Break-even count = first whole journey count where
T-money simple cost > Climate Card cost

This model deliberately keeps the first calculation simple, then stress-tests it against transfer discounts, distance-based fares, card purchase costs, and Climate Card coverage. It is a decision aid, not a promise that every traveler will pay exactly the modeled amount.

One more 2026 update

Seoul replaced the resident-focused 30-day Climate Card program with the new Climate Pass in September 2026, but the tourist short-term Climate Card remains available in 1-, 2-, 3-, 5-, and 7-day versions. Do not rely on older articles that imply the short-term tourist pass disappeared.
